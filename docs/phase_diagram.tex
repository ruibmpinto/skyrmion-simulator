\title{Phase Diagram of the SAF Skyrmion Simulator:\\
Theory, Reduced Units, and Implementation}
\author{Rui Barreira}
\date{May 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This note documents the theoretical content of the phase-diagram
subsystem added to the SAF skyrmion simulator. The original
simulator (see \texttt{docs/theory.tex}) folded the thin-film
magnetostatic correction into the effective anisotropy
$\Keff = K - \mu_0 \Ms^2 / 2$. The phase-diagram subsystem
replaces that contraction with an explicit two-dimensional
Fourier-space demag, introduces a corrected total-energy
functional that respects the bilinear vs.\ external prefactor
convention, and classifies relaxed textures into eight phases
using order parameters that combine layer-averaged
magnetization, topological charge, an interlayer dot product,
and the angular content of the Fourier spectrum at the
dominant wavevector. We also adopt reduced units
$(D / \Dc,\; H_z / \HK)$ on the phase-diagram axes so the
boundaries are material-independent, and we extend the
initial-condition ensemble with a \emph{parallel} ferromagnet
seed so the sweep can resolve the spin-flop transition that
the antiparallel manifold alone cannot expose.
\end{abstract}

%======================================================================
\section{Why a New Energy Functional}
%======================================================================

\subsection{The $\Keff$ shortcut}

The base simulator computes the effective field with a single
\emph{local} anisotropy term whose prefactor is
$\Keff = K - \mu_0 \Ms^2 / 2$, absorbing the leading thin-film
magnetostatic contribution into the bulk anisotropy. The
identity that justifies this is, for a thin film of in-plane
extent $\gg \tCo$ in the uniform limit,
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:keff_uniform}
\mathcal{H}_{\text{anis}} + \mathcal{H}_{\text{demag}}
    = -K \tCo \int m_z^2 \, dS
    + \frac{\mu_0 \Ms^2}{2} \, \tCo \int m_z^2 \, dS
    = -\Keff \, \tCo \int m_z^2 \, dS.
\end{equation}
%
For the uniform out-of-plane saturated state this is exact
and recovers the textbook shape anisotropy. For a textured
state with characteristic in-plane wavenumber $|\mb{k}| > 0$
the contraction \emph{fails}: the magnetostatic tensor in
Fourier space is not constant across $\mb{k}$, so the demag
contribution to a periodic texture differs from the uniform
prediction. Two consequences matter here:
%
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Textures with finite $|\mb{k}|$ generically gain energy
    relative to uniform FM, because the thin-film shape
    function is bounded by one:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:shape_function}
    f(|\mb{k}| \tCo) = \frac{1 - e^{-|\mb{k}|\tCo}}
        {|\mb{k}|\tCo} \le 1,
    \end{equation}
    with $f \to 1$ at $|\mb{k}|\tCo \to 0$ (the uniform
    limit) and $f \to 0$ at $|\mb{k}|\tCo \to \infty$. The
    $\Keff$ contraction overestimates the demag penalty of a
    short-wavelength state by a factor $1/f$.
\item In a synthetic antiferromagnet the two layers couple
    magnetostatically across the Ru spacer. The interlayer
    contribution vanishes at $\mb{k} = 0$ (an infinite
    uniformly magnetized slab produces no external field) but
    is finite at $|\mb{k}| > 0$ with decay $e^{-|\mb{k}|\dRu}$.
    A local-anisotropy model has no access to this term.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Explicit 2D Fourier demag}

Each Co layer is treated as a slab of thickness $\tCo$
embedded in vacuum with periodic in-plane boundary conditions.
The magnetization on layer $\ell$ is decomposed in Fourier
modes $\widetilde{\mb{m}}^{(\ell)}(\mb{k})$, and the demag
field on layer $\ell$ from both layers reads
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:demag_k}
\widetilde{\Hdemag}^{(\ell)}(\mb{k})
    = -\mu_0 \Ms \sum_{\ell'} \mb{N}^{(\ell, \ell')}(\mb{k})
        \cdot \widetilde{\mb{m}}^{(\ell')}(\mb{k}),
\end{equation}
%
with $\mb{N}^{(\ell, \ell')}(\mb{k})$ the dimensionless
$3 \times 3$ thin-film magnetostatic tensor. The diagonal
components used in the simulator are
%
\begin{align}
N_{xx}^{\text{self}}(\mb{k}) &= \frac{k_x^2}{k^2}
    \bigl(1 - f(k\tCo)\bigr), &
N_{yy}^{\text{self}}(\mb{k}) &= \frac{k_y^2}{k^2}
    \bigl(1 - f(k\tCo)\bigr), \nonumber\\
N_{zz}^{\text{self}}(\mb{k}) &= f(k\tCo), &
N_{xy}^{\text{self}}(\mb{k}) &= \frac{k_x k_y}{k^2}
    \bigl(1 - f(k\tCo)\bigr),
\label{eq:N_self}
\end{align}
%
with $k = |\mb{k}|$ and $f$ from~\eqref{eq:shape_function}.
The inter-layer tensor uses the same in-plane structure
multiplied by an exponentially decaying gap factor and an
overall sign flip on the out-of-plane component:
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:N_inter}
\mb{N}^{(\text{top, bot})}(\mb{k})
    = S(k) \begin{pmatrix}
        k_x^2 / k^2 & k_x k_y / k^2 & 0 \\
        k_x k_y / k^2 & k_y^2 / k^2 & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & -1
    \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
S(k) = \frac{(1 - e^{-k\tCo})^2}{(k\tCo)^2}
    e^{-k\dRu},
\end{equation}
%
and vanishes at $\mb{k} = 0$, consistent with an infinite
parallel-plate slab producing no field outside itself. In
the limit $k\tCo \to 0$ on the self-tensor only,
\eqref{eq:N_self} reduces to $N_{zz}^{\text{self}}(0) = 1$ and
all in-plane components vanish, recovering the uniform
shape-anisotropy result
$(\Hdemag)_z = -\mu_0 \Ms m_z$ and
hence the $\Keff$ relation~\eqref{eq:keff_uniform}.

\subsection{Bare anisotropy at finite $\mb{k}$}

Because the demag now appears explicitly, the bulk
anisotropy prefactor used by the LLGS effective field is
the \emph{bare} $K$, not $\Keff$:
%
\begin{equation}
\Hanis^{(\ell)} = \frac{2 K_\ell}{\Ms} m_z^{(\ell)} \, \zhat,
\qquad K_\ell \in \{K_{\text{top}}, K_{\text{bot}}\}.
\end{equation}
%
The original simulator stores $\Keff$ in its anisotropy
prefactor, so the phase-diagram code recomputes
$2 K_\ell / \Ms$ from the raw material constants $(K_\ell,
\Ms)$ on each parameter namespace. The legacy field path
that uses $\Keff$ is left untouched so existing
current-driven analyses remain unaffected.

\subsection{Corrected total energy}

For a system with effective field expressed in tesla
($\mb{H}_T = \mu_0 \, \mb{H}_{A/m}$), the per-cell
self-energy
of a bilinear contribution $\mb{H}$ and the Zeeman term are
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:E_density}
e_{\text{self}} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\, \Ms \,
    \mb{m} \cdot \mb{H}_T,
\qquad
e_Z = -\Ms \, \mb{m} \cdot \mb{H}_{\text{ext},T},
\end{equation}
%
without any additional $\mu_0$ prefactor because that
factor is already absorbed in the conversion from A/m to T.
The total energy summed over both Co layers therefore reads
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:E_total}
\boxed{\;
E = -\tfrac{1}{2}\, V \Ms \!
    \sum_{\ell} \sum_{i}
    \mb{m}_i^{(\ell)} \cdot \mb{H}_{\text{int},i}^{(\ell)}
    - V \Ms \!
    \sum_{\ell} \sum_{i}
    \mb{m}_i^{(\ell)} \cdot \mb{H}_{\text{ext},T}
\;}
\end{equation}
%
with $V = \tCo a^2$ the per-site volume of one layer and
$\mb{H}_{\text{int}} =
\Hex + \Hdmi + \Hanis^{\text{bare}} + \Hrkky + \Hdemag$. The
$\tfrac{1}{2}$ prefactor prevents double counting of the
bilinear self-interactions; it is absent on the Zeeman term
because the external field is independent of the
magnetization. Substituting
$\Hanis^{\text{bare}} = (2K/\Ms) m_z \zhat$ recovers the
textbook density $-K m_z^2$, confirming the convention.

%======================================================================
\section{Reduced Units}
%======================================================================

A phase diagram in absolute $(D,\, H_z)$ collapses to a
single universal picture once the axes are normalized by the
material's natural scales. We adopt:
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reduced}
\boxed{\;
x \equiv \frac{D}{\Dc},
\qquad
y \equiv \frac{H_z}{\HK},
\;}
\end{equation}
%
with $\Dc$ the Bogdanov--Hubert critical DMI and $\HK$ the
anisotropy field. Both depend only on the material; they are
computed once per sweep and stored as scalars in the output.

\subsection{Critical DMI $\Dc$ (Bogdanov--Hubert)}

The uniform PMA ferromagnet is locally stable against
spontaneous helical winding only below
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dc}
\Dc = \frac{4 \sqrt{\Aex \, \Keff}}{\pi},
\qquad
\Keff = \tfrac{1}{2}(K_{\text{top}} + K_{\text{bot}})
    - \tfrac{1}{2} \mu_0 \Ms^2.
\end{equation}
%
Above $\Dc$ the FM gains energy by canting into a
periodic helix; below it the FM is the ground state at
$H_z = 0$. The factor $4/\pi$ is the saddle-point result
for a sinusoidal spiral on an infinite plane with no
anisotropy in the easy-plane direction. We use the
layer-averaged $\Keff$ to fold the small asymmetry between
$K_{\text{top}}$ and $K_{\text{bot}}$ into a single scalar.
The formula is undefined for $\Keff \le 0$; the
implementation raises an exception in that easy-plane
regime, since no PMA FM exists to destabilize.

\subsection{Anisotropy field $\HK$}

The anisotropy field is the field at which the Zeeman
energy of a uniform out-of-plane ferromagnet equals the
anisotropy gap. In A/m units the textbook expression is
$\HK = 2 \Keff / (\mu_0 \Ms)$. The simulator's convention
keeps every field in tesla, so we store
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HK}
\HK^T = \mu_0 \HK = \frac{2 \Keff}{\Ms}
    \quad \text{(tesla)}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{PMA quality factor}

The dimensionless ratio
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qpma}
\Qpma = \frac{\Keff}{\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_0 \Ms^2}
\end{equation}
%
sets which regime the material is in.
Table~\ref{tab:Qpma_regimes} summarizes the qualitative
behaviour for the SAF system; we obtain
$\Qpma \approx 0.007$ at $K = 1.294 \times 10^6$ J/m$^3$
(default) and $\Qpma \approx 0.245$ at
$K = 1.60 \times 10^6$ J/m$^3$ (the value used in this
work to access the chiral regime).
%

\subsection{Material constants for the present sweep}

For $K = 1.60 \times 10^6$ J/m$^3$ in both layers and the
default $\Ms = 1.43$ MA/m, $\Aex = 16$ pJ/m, we obtain:
%
\begin{equation*}
\Keff \approx 0.315\ \text{MJ/m}^3,
\qquad
\Dc \approx 2.86\ \text{mJ/m}^2,
\qquad
\HK \approx 0.441\ \text{T},
\qquad
\Qpma \approx 0.245.
\end{equation*}
%
With $D$ swept over $[0, 4]$ mJ/m$^2$ and $H_z$ over
$[-0.5, +0.5]$ T, the reduced-unit coverage is
$x \in [0, 1.40]$ and $y \in [-1.13, +1.13]$, straddling
both the chiral threshold $x = 1$ and the SAF spin-flop
$|y| = \HRKKY / \HK \approx 0.47$. The upper $D$ bound is
chosen to keep the skyrmion core radius
$R_{sk} \sim \Delta_{DW} \sim \sqrt{\Aex / \Keff}$ above
the lattice resolution; pushing further would require
$a < 1$~nm. Section~\ref{sec:Hz_redundancy} discusses
alternative sweeps that replace the $H_z$ axis.

%======================================================================
\section{Phase Classification}
%======================================================================

A relaxed configuration is labelled by a decision tree on
five order parameters. The labels are
$\{\text{FM\_anti}, \text{FM\_par+}, \text{FM\_par-},
\text{iSk}, \text{SkX}, \text{BX}, \text{SS},
\text{Lab}\}$ plus a sentinel \texttt{undetermined}.

\subsection{Order parameters}

For the top layer (the bottom layer is RKKY-coupled and
follows it up to canting):
%
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Layer-averaged out-of-plane magnetization
    $\langle m_z \rangle$.
\item Layer dot product
    $\langle \mb{m}_{\text{top}} \cdot
    \mb{m}_{\text{bot}} \rangle$, the interlayer
    Néel order parameter. Cleanly antiparallel SAF
    states satisfy this $\approx -1$, parallel
    (spin-flopped) FM states satisfy $\approx +1$.
\item Topological charge of the top layer,
    $Q = \frac{1}{4\pi} \int \mb{m} \cdot
    (\partial_x \mb{m} \times \partial_y \mb{m}) \, dA$.
\item Power spectrum of $m_z(\mb{r})$. Two summary
    statistics are computed: a peak-to-background ratio
    on the radial spectrum (used to gate the
    ``periodic'' branch) and the
    2-fold and 6-fold angular harmonics of the azimuthal
    profile on a Gaussian ring of width
    $\sigma = 1.5\, \Delta k_{\text{grid}}$ around the
    dominant in-plane wavevector $k^\star$.
\item Topological charge per principal period,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:q_per_period}
    q_p \equiv \frac{|Q|}{N_p},
    \qquad
    N_p \equiv
    \frac{k^\star L_x}{2\pi} \cdot
    \frac{k^\star L_y}{2\pi}.
    \end{equation}
    A hexagonal skyrmion lattice has $q_p \approx 1$
    (one full unit of charge per unit cell); a bubble
    lattice with the same FFT signature but trivial
    topology has $q_p \approx 0$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Decision tree}

Let $|\langle m_z \rangle| > 0.95$ mean ``uniform'',
$|\langle \mb{m}_{\text{top}} \cdot \mb{m}_{\text{bot}}
\rangle| > 0.9$ mean ``clean polar'', and
$P_6 / P_{\text{iso}} > 2$ with
$P_6 > P_2$ mean ``6-fold periodic'' (analogously for SS).
The classifier proceeds:
%
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item If uniform: pick FM by interlayer dot product.
    Antiparallel ($\langle \mb{m}_{\text{top}} \cdot
    \mb{m}_{\text{bot}} \rangle < -0.9$) yields
    \textbf{FM\_anti}; parallel yields
    \textbf{FM\_par+} or \textbf{FM\_par-} based on the
    sign of $\langle m_z \rangle$. The uniform check is
    \emph{and}-coupled with the dot-product check so a
    textured antiparallel state with $\langle
    \mb{m}_{\text{top}} \cdot \mb{m}_{\text{bot}}
    \rangle \approx -1$ but
    $|\langle m_z \rangle| < 1$ falls through.
\item If $|Q| \in [0.5, 1.5]$ (one or a few isolated
    skyrmions, broad FFT): \textbf{iSk}.
\item If a 6-fold periodic ring is present:
    \textbf{SkX} when $q_p \ge 0.5$,
    \textbf{BX} otherwise.
\item If a 2-fold periodic ring is present:
    \textbf{SS} (spin spiral / helix).
\item If a periodic ring with no clean angular order:
    \textbf{Lab} (labyrinthine).
\item Fallbacks: $|Q| > 1.5$ without periodic ordering
    $\to$ \textbf{iSk} (multi-skyrmion family);
    $|Q| < 0.5$ without ordering $\to$ \textbf{Lab};
    else \textbf{undetermined}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why SkX vs.\ BX matters}

Both phases have 6-fold periodic $m_z(\mb{r})$ in the
FFT. They differ in whether each periodic minimum is
topologically protected. A real skyrmion lattice has
$|Q| = N_{\text{sk}}$ skyrmions per field of view, so
$q_p \to 1$. A bubble lattice (or a stripe / labyrinth
pattern with hexagonal symmetry) carries no winding and
$q_p \to 0$. At zero DMI no real SkX can exist by
construction; any 6-fold periodic ground state at
$D = 0$ is therefore BX. Conflating the two would
falsely report a chiral phase across regions of the
$(D, H_z)$ plane that are in fact demag-driven and
topologically trivial.

\section{Initial-Condition Ensemble}
%======================================================================

At each grid point the relaxation is run from twelve
initial conditions; the lowest-energy converged result is
taken as the ground state. The ensemble is designed to
seed every basin we know to be physically relevant. Every
IC returns an antiparallel SAF pair
($\mb{m}_{\text{bot}} = -\mb{m}_{\text{top}}$ cell by
cell) so the RKKY ground state is respected at the start
of relaxation, except for \texttt{fm\_par}, which
deliberately seeds the parallel-locked manifold.
%
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \texttt{random} $\times 5$ (seeds 11, 22, 33, 44, 55):
    independent uniform-on-the-sphere spin fields
    ($\varphi \sim U[0, 2\pi]$, $\cos\theta \sim U[-1, 1]$)
    to explore the texture manifold without geometric
    bias. Five seeds give better statistical coverage
    of the disordered basins than the previous three.
\item \texttt{fm\_anti}: antiparallel SAF
    ($\mb{m}_{\text{top}} = \pm\zhat$,
    $\mb{m}_{\text{bot}} = \mp\zhat$). Tracks the FM
    branch of the antiparallel manifold.
\item \texttt{fm\_par}: parallel FM
    ($\mb{m}_{\text{top}} = \mb{m}_{\text{bot}} =
    \text{sign}(H_z) \zhat$). Required to expose the
    $H_z$-driven antiparallel $\to$ parallel spin-flop.
\item \texttt{skyrmion}: a single N\'{e}el SAF skyrmion
    (\texttt{saf\_skyrmion} from
    \texttt{src/skyrmion\_simulator/simulator/initial\_conditions.py}). Seeds
    the iSk basin.
\item \texttt{stripe}: a 1D helical stripe along $\hat{x}$
    of pitch $\lambda = 4\pi \Aex / D$ (the natural DMI
    helix period), floored at $4a$.
\item \texttt{stripe\_y}: same helix along $\hat{y}$;
    probes orientation degeneracy of the SS phase and
    commensurability with the box.
\item \texttt{sk\_lattice}: a hexagonal lattice of
    N\'{e}el skyrmions with nearest-neighbour distance
    equal to $\lambda$. Each grid cell picks up the
    profile of its nearest hex centre with radial in-plane
    winding (Q = $\pm 1$ per core). Without this seed the
    sweep almost never finds the SkX basin from a random
    start at $256 \times 256$.
\item \texttt{bubble\_lattice}: identical hex sites and
    $m_z$ profile as \texttt{sk\_lattice}, but with the
    in-plane direction held fixed along $+\hat{x}$ (no
    winding). Each bubble carries $Q = 0$; the IC seeds
    the BX basin and provides the classifier's
    null-topology counterpart to verify the per-period
    $q_p$ discriminator.
\end{itemize}

The IC builders are in
\texttt{src/skyrmion\_simulator/phase\_diagram/initial\_conditions\_ext.py}.
Figure~\ref{fig:ic_gallery} renders each IC at one
representative point in the phase plane along with the
classifier's verdict on the unrelaxed state.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering

$D = D_c = 2.86$~mJ/m$^2$, $H_z = 0$, $K = 1.60$~MJ/m$^3$

\subsection{Zero-Zeeman SAF and the spin-flop}

Inside the antiparallel manifold ($\mb{m}_{\text{top}} =
-\mb{m}_{\text{bot}}$ cell by cell) the Zeeman density is
%
\begin{equation}
e_Z = -\Ms (\mb{m}_{\text{top}} +
    \mb{m}_{\text{bot}}) \cdot \mb{H}_{\text{ext}, T}
    \equiv 0,
\end{equation}
%
identically, regardless of $|H_z|$. The applied field is
decoupled from every state inside this manifold, including
FM\_anti, BX, SkX, iSk, SS, Lab. A genuine $H_z$-driven
phase boundary therefore requires the parallel manifold,
which becomes energetically favorable above the spin-flop:
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spinflop}
|H_z| \gtrsim \HRKKY \approx 0.205\ \text{T},
\end{equation}
%
where the Zeeman gain from aligning both layers exceeds
the RKKY cost of breaking antiparallel coupling. The
\texttt{fm\_par} seed allows the relaxation to find this
basin; without it the sweep would only sample
antiparallel-manifold states and the high-$|y|$ regions
of the phase diagram would be incorrectly attributed to
the antiparallel FM rather than to the parallel FM.

%======================================================================
\section{Numerical Workflow}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Sweep}

The sweep iterates over a grid of two configurable axes
$(\text{axis}_x, \text{axis}_y)$ chosen from the registry
\texttt{src/skyrmion\_simulator/phase\_diagram/axis\_specs.py::axes}.
Registered axes are
$D, H_z, K_{\text{top}}, \HRKKY, \Ms, \Aex, \tCo, \dRu,
\alpha$; the typical pairs are $(D, H_z)$, $(D, K_{\text{top}})$,
$(D, \HRKKY)$. The grid itself is an arbitrary 1-D numpy
array per axis -- uniform \texttt{linspace} is the
default but non-uniform clustering around critical lines
(spin-flop, $\Dc$) is supported with no code changes.

For each grid point and each of the twelve ICs the
relaxation runs an RK4 LLGS integrator with $J = 0$ and
optional over-damped Gilbert factor $\alpha_{\text{relax}}
= 1$ until both convergence criteria fire:
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conv}
\max_i \bigl| \mb{m}_i \times
    (\mb{m}_i \times \Heff(\mb{r}_i)) \bigr|
    < \tau_{\text{tol}},
\qquad
\left| \frac{\Delta E}{E} \right| < \epsilon_E,
\end{equation}
%
checked every $10^3$ steps. The
\texttt{relaxation.relax} function defaults are
$\tau_{\text{tol}} = 10^{-5}$ T, $\epsilon_E = 10^{-8}$,
safety cutoff $2 \times 10^5$ steps. The running sweep
config in \texttt{sweep.main()} overrides these to
$\tau_{\text{tol}} = 10^{-3}$ T and
$\epsilon_E = 10^{-7}$ with a $10^5$-step cutoff: the
torque tolerance is loosened to about
$2 \times 10^{-3}$ in $\HK$ units, comfortably above the
amplitude of long-lived breathing modes
($\sim 10^{-3}$~T) but eleven orders of magnitude above
the float64 precision floor ($\sim 10^{-15}$~T), so the
relaxation no longer reports false-negatives at the cost
of $\sim 10^{-3}$ relative uncertainty in $\langle m_z
\rangle$. The tangential-torque criterion measures
$|\mb{m} \times (\mb{m} \times \Heff)|$ rather than
$|\dot{\mb{m}}|$ to avoid contamination from any
$|\mb{m}| \ne 1$ numerical drift.

The lowest-energy converged IC at each grid point is taken
as the ground state, and per-IC observables plus the
ground-state texture are written to a single compressed
NPZ. SLURM-array mode (triggered by
\texttt{SLURM\_ARRAY\_TASK\_ID}) writes one partial NPZ
per element; \texttt{aggregate.py} combines them in strict
mode (cross-partial consistency of axes, IC list, and
material scalars is checked; any mismatch raises).

\subsection{Reduced-unit plotting}

The plotter defaults to $(D / \Dc, H_z / \HK)$ axes. The
critical-DMI vertical line $x = 1$ and the spin-flop
$|y| = \HRKKY / \HK$ are drawn on the phase map for
reference. Absolute units (mJ/m$^2$, T) are available as a
fallback.

\subsection{Classifier verification}

The classifier is verified on synthetic textures whose
labels are known by construction:
%
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Uniform $(\pm\zhat, \mp\zhat)$ $\to$ FM\_anti.
\item Uniform $(\pm\zhat, \pm\zhat)$ $\to$ FM\_par$\pm$.
\item Helical stripe at the natural pitch $\to$ SS.
\item Sum of three plane waves at $0$, $60$, $120$
    degrees with N\'{e}el winding $\to$ SkX (with $q_p$
    above threshold) or BX (without).
\end{itemize}

\section{Relevance to the Pham 2024 Experiment}
\label{sec:pham_value}
%======================================================================

A T=0 $(D, H_z)$ phase diagram for the Pt/Co/Ru/Pt/Co/Ru
stack of~\cite{pham2024} would mostly confirm what the
experiment already exploits, but it adds three quantitative
contributions that are invisible from a single-point
measurement.

\subsection{What it confirms (low scientific value)}

The paper operates at one
$(D, H_z, \Keff, \HRKKY)$ point and demonstrates that the
iSk texture is stable enough to drive at SOT-relevant
current densities. The diagram inside the antiparallel
manifold shows iSk occupying a band around their operating
point, FM\_anti at low $D$, SS/SkX at higher $D$, and
BX/Lab at marginal $\Keff$. None of this is surprising; it
is the standard chiral-magnet picture inherited
from~\cite{sampaio2013}, \cite{rohart2013}, and
\cite{buttner2018}, ported to the antiparallel SAF.

\subsection{What it adds (genuine value)}

\paragraph{1.\ The spin-flop sets the real upper limit, not
the anisotropy field.}
The paper's whole compensated-Hall claim rests on the two
Co layers being antiparallel. Above
$|H_z| \approx \HRKKY \approx 0.205$~T the layers
spin-flop to parallel, the skyrmion's bottom-layer partner
inverts, and the topological Hall force no longer cancels.
A conventional ferromagnetic phase diagram has no analogue
of this transition; the spin-flop is specific to the SAF,
and the diagram makes it the load-bearing constraint on
device operating range. The paper does not state where the
compensation fails as $|H_z|$ increases; the diagram
answers that directly through the
\texttt{FM\_anti}\,$\to$\,\texttt{FM\_par$\pm$} boundary at
$|y| \approx \HRKKY/\HK \approx 0.47$ for the parameters
in Section~2.

\paragraph{2.\ iSk vs.\ BX discriminator at marginal
$\Keff$.}
For the Pt/Co/Ru/Pt/Co stack in~\cite{pham2024},
$\Keff \approx 10^4$~J/m$^3$ --- barely positive. Both
DMI-driven N\'{e}el skyrmions and demag-driven bubble
domains can sit at the same operating point in
textures-per-area, and a magnetic-microscopy image alone
cannot tell them apart. The
topological-charge-per-period observable
$q_p = |Q|/N_p$ defined in~\eqref{eq:q_per_period} is the
cleanest discriminator: $q_p \to 1$ for genuine chiral
skyrmion lattices, $q_p \to 0$ for bubble lattices with
the same FFT signature. The diagram identifies which
$(D, H_z, \Keff)$ cells produce $Q = 1$ chiral cores
versus $Q = 0$ bubbles, and the experimental stack sits
exactly in the region where this discrimination matters.
The diagram would let the paper defend the
``skyrmion'' interpretation against the ``bubble''
alternative quantitatively, rather than by N\'{e}el-helicity
inspection alone.

\paragraph{3.\ Material design space for follow-on
devices.}
\cite{pham2024} reports skyrmion velocities up to
$\sim 900$~m/s at one specific stack. The phase diagram
gives the $(D, \Keff, \HRKKY)$ recipe for staying inside
the iSk basin:
%
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Smaller skyrmions: push $D$ up while staying below
    the SS boundary at $D/\Dc \approx 1$.
\item Larger skyrmions: lower $D$ toward (but not into)
    the FM\_anti region.
\item More robust against $H_z$: raise $\Keff$ and
    $\HRKKY$ proportionally so $\HK \propto \Keff/\Ms$
    and the spin-flop field both move outward.
\item Denser arrays: cross into the SkX region at higher
    $D/\Dc$ (just above $1$) where ground-state
    nucleation is automatic.
\end{itemize}
%
That recipe is a contribution the paper cannot make from a
single measurement.

\subsection{What it does not address}

The diagram is a T=0 ground-state characterization. It
does not predict skyrmion velocities, current thresholds,
SOT efficiency, or the thermal nucleation and annihilation
lifetimes that determine device reliability --- all of
which are the paper's actual experimental claims. The
diagram is therefore complementary, not competing: it
tells you \emph{where} the texture lives, not \emph{how}
it moves.

\subsection{Honest verdict}

For~\cite{pham2024} specifically, a $(D, H_z)$ diagram in
absolute units is mostly confirmatory: the iSk window is
the expected band, $\HRKKY$ is the spin-flop. The added
value is the iSk-vs-BX call at the experimental
$\Keff$ and the material-engineering map for follow-on
work. The diagram becomes more informative if a second
axis ($\Keff$ or $\HRKKY$ instead of $H_z$, which is
partly redundant inside the antiparallel manifold) is
swept; that would chart the actually sensitive parameters
and is the natural extension for materials-engineering
applications.

\section{Redundancy of the $H_z$ Axis and Alternative Sweeps}
\label{sec:Hz_redundancy}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Why $H_z$ is partly redundant}

Inside the antiparallel manifold
($\mb{m}_{\text{bot}}(\mb{r}) = -\mb{m}_{\text{top}}(\mb{r})$
cell-by-cell, locked by RKKY), the Zeeman energy density
is identically zero:
%
\begin{equation}
e_Z = -\Ms \bigl(\mb{m}_{\text{top}}
    + \mb{m}_{\text{bot}}\bigr) \cdot \mb{H}_{\text{ext}}
    \equiv 0
\qquad \text{for all } H_z,\ \text{all } \mb{r}.
\end{equation}

Two distinct consequences follow:

\paragraph{1.\ Energy degeneracy.}
The total energy of every antiparallel-manifold state ---
\texttt{FM\_anti}, \texttt{iSk}, \texttt{SkX},
\texttt{BX}, \texttt{SS}, \texttt{Lab} --- is independent
of $H_z$. The energy ordering between these states is also
independent of $H_z$. Consequently the ground-state label
at $(D, H_{z,1})$ is identical to the ground-state label
at $(D, H_{z,2})$ as long as both $H_z$ values are below
the spin-flop. Along the $H_z$ axis the diagram carries no
new information across that entire range.

\paragraph{2.\ Dynamics still see the field.}
Per-layer the LLGS torque $\mb{m} \times \mb{H}_{\text{ext}}$
is nonzero --- $H_z$ tries to flip $\mb{m}_{\text{bot}}$
toward $+\zhat$. What prevents this is the RKKY
counter-torque from $\mb{m}_{\text{top}}$. Below
$|H_z| \approx \HRKKY \approx 0.205$~T the RKKY wins and
the antiparallel state survives; above, it does not.
$H_z$ therefore controls a single binary degree of freedom:
\emph{is the system inside or outside the antiparallel
manifold?}

\medskip\noindent
Combining the two: the $H_z$ axis of a $(D, H_z)$ phase
diagram is mostly degenerate, resolving exactly one sharp
transition (the spin-flop) separating an antiparallel
band (where $H_z$ is irrelevant) from a parallel band
(where $H_z$ behaves as in a conventional ferromagnet).
Most of the computational cost of the sweep is spent on
the degenerate direction, and the only $H_z$-dependent
feature the diagram resolves is the spin-flop line at
%
\begin{equation}
\left|\frac{H_z}{\HK}\right| \approx
    \frac{\HRKKY}{\HK} \approx 0.47
\qquad \text{(for } K = 1.60\ \text{MJ/m}^3\text{).}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Alternative sweep directions}

Three sweep directions place the computational effort on
parameters that are not degenerate in the antiparallel
manifold.

\paragraph{$(D, \Keff)$ --- chiral-magnet phase plane.}
The natural chiral-magnet phase diagram of the
antiparallel manifold. At fixed $\Keff$, increasing $D$
walks through \texttt{FM\_anti}~$\to$~\texttt{iSk}~$\to$~
\texttt{SkX}~$\to$~\texttt{SS}. At fixed $D$, decreasing
$\Keff$ walks through \texttt{FM\_anti}~$\to$~
\texttt{iSk/SkX}~$\to$~\texttt{BX}~$\to$~\texttt{Lab}, with
anisotropy losing to magnetostatics. In reduced units the
plane is
%
\begin{equation}
\left( \frac{D}{\Dc(\Keff)},\;
       \Qpma \right)
\qquad \text{with } \Qpma =
    \frac{\Keff}{\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_0 \Ms^2},
\end{equation}
%
and the phase boundaries are universal across SAF
materials with the same Bogdanov--Hubert scaling.
For~\cite{pham2024} the operating point sits at
$\Qpma \approx 0.007$, right at the marginal-PMA
boundary; a $(D, \Keff)$ sweep quantifies the smallest
engineering change in $\Keff$ (interface anisotropy, Co
thickness) that moves the system out of the BX-risk
region and into a clean \texttt{iSk} band.

\paragraph{$(D, \HRKKY)$ --- SAF-specific complement.}
This sweep probes the axis that is genuinely
SAF-specific. At small $\HRKKY$ the two Co layers
decouple and the SAF degenerates into two weakly-coupled
ferromagnets, restoring the conventional skyrmion Hall
effect that the SAF was designed to suppress. At large
$\HRKKY$ the layers are rigidly locked and the SAF is
robust, but the spin-flop field also moves outward,
narrowing the operating window in $|H_z|$. The diagram
expresses the trade-off between mechanical robustness of
the antiparallel locking and the maximum tolerable
$|H_z|$. The Pham experiment fixes one Ru thickness
(one $\HRKKY$); a $(D, \HRKKY)$ map would show whether
thinner or thicker Ru gives a wider \texttt{iSk} window,
which is one of the most important materials-engineering
knobs in a SAF skyrmion device.

\paragraph{$(\Keff, \HRKKY)$ at fixed $D$ --- pure design
space.}
Drops the chirality axis to display the
materials-engineering plane directly. Use this once a
target skyrmion size has been fixed via the helical pitch
$\lambda_h = 4\pi \Aex / D$. The diagram is then a
``what stack should I grow?'' map, with axes that the
materials chemist actually controls (interface anisotropy
through Co/heavy-metal thickness; interlayer coupling
through Ru thickness).

\section{Implementation Notes}
\label{sec:impl}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Generic axis registry}

The sweep is axis-agnostic. Each entry in
\texttt{src/skyrmion\_simulator/phase\_diagram/axis\_specs.py::axes} declares a
display label, a display scale (SI $\to$ plot units), and
an override-builder that turns one axis value into a
\texttt{make\_params(**overrides)} kwarg dict. Adding a new
sweep axis means appending one entry; the sweep, partial
writer, aggregator, and plotter pick it up without further
changes. Strict mode: unknown axis names, empty axis
arrays, and material-scalar mismatches across partials all
raise \texttt{RuntimeError}. Reduced-unit plotting
$(D / \Dc, H_z / \HK)$ is supported only for the
$(D, H_z)$ axis pair; other pairs render in absolute units
from the registry.

\subsection{Non-uniform axis sampling}

The grid arrays in \texttt{sweep.main()} are plain numpy
1-D arrays. The production sweep uses
\textbf{clustered sampling} around the physical
transitions instead of a uniform \texttt{linspace}: 30
points along $D$ concentrated around $\Dc$, 30 points
along $H_z$ concentrated around $|H_z| = \HRKKY$. The 10
densest $D$ points fall in $\Dc \pm 0.2$~mJ/m$^2$ where
the chiral phase boundaries actually live; the 9 densest
$H_z$ points lie in a 0.1-T window around each spin-flop
transition. The non-uniform grid resolves both critical
lines $\sim 2\times$ better than a uniform 30-point grid
at the same task count.

\subsection{Lattice refinement}

$\Delta_{DW} = \sqrt{\Aex / \Keff} \approx 7.13$~nm at
$K = 1.60$~MJ/m$^3$. The original simulator used
$a = 2$~nm, giving $\Delta_{DW} / a \approx 3.5$ -- below
the standard micromagnetic floor of $\ge 7$ cells per
domain wall. The phase-diagram sweep overrides $a = 1$~nm
(and rescales $dt$ as $1/a^2$ for RK4 stability), giving
$\Delta_{DW} / a = 7$ cells. The lattice constant is
overridable per task via the \texttt{a} and \texttt{dt}
fields of \texttt{make\_params}; the demag kernel
precompute reads $p.a$ at construction time.

\subsection{Companion scripts}

Two visualization scripts live alongside the sweep
pipeline:
%
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \texttt{studies/saf\_racetrack/phase\_diagram/show\_ics.py}: render the
    IC ensemble at one $(D, H_z, K)$ point and classify
    each IC's \emph{unrelaxed} state. Output:
    \texttt{output/phase\_diagram/ic\_gallery.png}.
    Useful for comparing initial vs. final labels in a
    real sweep cell. See Figure~\ref{fig:ic_gallery}.
\item \texttt{studies/saf\_racetrack/phase\_diagram/show\_phases.py}: render
    a schematic phase diagram in reduced units
    $(D/\Dc, H_z/\HK)$ with annotated regions and IC
    target positions. Output:
    \texttt{output/phase\_diagram/schematic\_phase\_diagram.png}.
    Useful as a teaching reference and as a comparison
    baseline against real sweep output. See
    Figure~\ref{fig:schematic_phase}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Strict-mode aggregator}

\texttt{src/skyrmion\_simulator/phase\_diagram/aggregate.py} combines SLURM
array partials. Every partial carries the canonical IC
list (\texttt{ic\_names\_full}, \texttt{ic\_seeds\_full}),
the two axis arrays (\texttt{axis\_x\_grid},
\texttt{axis\_y\_grid}), and the material scalars
($\Ms$, $\Aex$, $\mu_0$, $\HRKKY$,
$K_{\text{top}}$, $K_{\text{bot}}$, $\tCo$, $\dRu$, $a$).
The aggregator reads them from the first partial, requires
every subsequent partial to agree exactly, and raises
\texttt{RuntimeError} on any mismatch. There is no legacy
fallback: partials missing a required key are rejected.

\section{Summary}
%======================================================================

The phase-diagram subsystem replaces the $\Keff$ thin-film
contraction with an explicit 2D Fourier-space demag
\eqref{eq:demag_k}--\eqref{eq:N_inter}, restores the bare
anisotropy prefactor at the LLGS effective field, and writes
total energies through the bilinear-vs.-external convention
\eqref{eq:E_density}--\eqref{eq:E_total}. Phase diagrams are
expressed in the dimensionless coordinates
$(D/\Dc, H_z/\HK)$ defined by~\eqref{eq:Dc}--\eqref{eq:HK}
for the canonical $(D, H_z)$ axis pair, and in absolute
SI units for any other pair selected from the
\texttt{axis\_specs.axes} registry
(Section~\ref{sec:impl}). The classifier's decision tree
distinguishes topologically non-trivial skyrmion lattices
from trivial bubble lattices via the per-period charge
$q_p$~\eqref{eq:q_per_period}, and the relaxation is run
from a 12-member IC ensemble that includes explicit seeds
for FM ($\times 2$), iSk ($\times 1$), SS ($\times 2$
orientations), SkX ($\times 1$), BX ($\times 1$), plus
five random spheres for statistical coverage. The choice
$K = 1.60$ MJ/m$^3$ places the material at
$\Qpma \approx 0.25$, in the regime where chiral phases
are expected; the absolute sweep ranges $D \in [0, 4]$
mJ/m$^2$ and $H_z \in [-0.5, +0.5]$ T translate to
$D/\Dc \in [0, 1.40]$ and $H_z/\HK \in [-1.13, +1.13]$,
discretized as a (30, 30) clustered grid around the
spin-flop and the chiral threshold.
